Design intent, explained

Design intent is the plan behind a model: which sizes drive the others, what stays centered, what must never change. Build it in and the part updates the way you expect. Leave it out and the next edit breaks it.

Two parts that look the same

Take a plate with a hole in the middle, modeled two ways:

  • Version A: the hole is dimensioned 50 mm from the left edge. Make the plate 140 mm long and the hole stays 50 mm from the left. It's no longer centered.
  • Version B: the hole is tied to the plate's center, with the sketch centered on the origin. Make the plate 140 mm long and the hole is still in the middle.

Both rebuild without errors and look identical. Only B captures what the designer meant: the hole is centered. That's design intent, and it only shows up when someone changes something.

Where intent lives in SolidWorks

ToolWhat it capturesExample
Sketch relationsGeometric rulesMidpoint, Symmetric, Equal, Collinear, Tangent
DimensionsWhat's measured from whereDimension a hole from the center line, not from an edge that may move
Origin and planesThe part's frame of referenceCenter the base sketch on the origin, so mirrors and symmetry come free
End conditionsHow a feature reacts to size changesThrough All for a through hole, not a blind depth that matches today's thickness
Equations and global variablesRules between sizesWall = Height / 10
Feature orderWhat depends on whatFillets after the features they round; pattern the feature, not its sketch
Configurations and design tablesFamilies of sizesOne file for the M6, M8 and M10 versions

Good habits

  • Fully define sketches. Blue geometry can move when something upstream changes.
  • Relate to the origin and the main planes, not to edges that might change.
  • Use a relation when the rule is geometric. Equal, symmetric and centered say more than two matching numbers.
  • Name what matters. "Wall Thickness" beats D1@Boss-Extrude1 for the next person, and for you in six months.
  • Pick end conditions that say what you mean, such as Through All for a through hole.
  • Keep fillets and cosmetic features late in the tree.
  • Test it. Change the main sizes up and down and rebuild. If something breaks, fix it now, not when a customer asks for a longer one.

You
The bore must stay centered on the plate when the length changes. How should it be constrained?

Caddie

Constrain the bore center to the midpoint of a construction diagonal between opposite plate corners. That keeps it centered as the plate’s length or width changes.

The stress test

The best check of design intent is to change things and see what breaks. Since version 0.3.1 Caddie can do that on the open part: it makes each driving dimension 20% bigger, then 20% smaller, rebuilds after each change, lists every feature that fails or warns, and puts every value back. On a test part it checked 17 dimensions in under 7 seconds. The graphics and the tree pause while it runs, and nothing is saved.

Bracket.SLDPRT · six driving dimensions
You
Will this part still rebuild if the main dimensions change?
Caddie

Not reliably: Mounting Hole fails when the 40 mm Base Profile dimension is reduced by 20%. The other five driving dimensions rebuilt at both 20% larger and smaller, and all values are restored.

I couldn’t inspect the hole’s references, but I’d first check whether its position leaves enough material when the plate narrows.

It works on parts, up to 20 driving dimensions or 30 seconds per run. Close any open sketch first.
