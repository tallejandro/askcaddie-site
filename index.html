How it works

It reads the model, so you don't have to explain it.

A general chatbot only knows what you type or paste into it. Caddie reads SolidWorks directly every time you ask, so you never have to describe the model first.

1

It reads the open document

In a fraction of a second, before it answers:

  • The feature tree, with rebuild errors and warnings
  • Sketch status, relations and what's over-defining a sketch
  • Dimensions, equations and configurations
  • Material, mass and center of mass
  • Measured checks: hole depth against the material, holes near edges and bends, thin walls, pocket corners, bend radii and draft
  • Sheet metal settings, weldment cut lists and Hole Wizard holes
  • Assembly components and mates, including which parts can still move, and drawing sheets and views
  • A screenshot of the window, unless you turn screenshots off
2

You ask as you would a colleague

There's no need to name the feature or copy an error message. Ask "Why won't this part rebuild?" and Caddie checks the model before it replies, so the answer is about your part rather than parts in general. Put files designed together in a project, and Caddie carries the requirements and decisions from one conversation to the next.

3

It explains, guides you, or does it

Answers name the feature and the value, and explain as much as you need: tell Caddie whether you're learning SolidWorks or use it every day. When there's work to do, Caddie starts a guide that highlights each click, or proposes the exact change for you to approve.

Guides

It points at the exact button, menu or field.

Ask how to do something and Caddie starts a short guide. Each step highlights where to click in SolidWorks, and ticks itself off when SolidWorks shows it's done, so you're never hunting through menus. You make every click yourself.

  • Highlights ribbon buttons, menus, PropertyManager fields and FeatureManager items
  • Moves to the next step on its own as you work
  • Highlight it again, skip a step, or stop the guide at any time

Changes you approve

It can make the fix, after you say yes.

When the fix is clear, Caddie shows exactly what it will change. Nothing happens until you click Do it, and most changes come with an Undo button.

  • Sketches, extrudes and cuts, fillets, chamfers, shells, mirrors and patterns
  • Dimensions, materials, names, suppression and custom properties
  • If any step fails, everything is put back

Attach an image

Show it a drawing, sketch or photo.

Paste, drop or attach an image of what you want to model. Caddie works out the shape and the dimensions, says what it assumed, and either builds it in steps you approve or guides you through building it yourself.

  • Drawings, hand sketches, screenshots and photos
  • Says what it assumed, so you can correct it
  • You approve each step before it's built

Specific answers, word for word.

Unedited answers from Caddie's test runs on sample models. Each one names the feature and the number, and says what's worth checking.

Sheet metalCover.SLDPRT
What blank size do I need for this part?

The flat blank is 182.6 × 95 mm in 1.5 mm AISI 304 sheet.

That size uses the model’s K-factor of 0.44, so confirm the bend allowance with your fabricator before cutting.

WeldmentsFrame.SLDPRT
What's the cut list for the square tubing?

The square tubing cut list for 30 × 30 × 2.60 mm tube is:

  • 4 pieces at 630 mm, with 45° cuts at both ends
  • 4 pieces at 970 mm, with square cuts at both ends
Calculationworked exactly
What's the maximum shear stress in a 20 mm solid steel shaft carrying 50 N·m of torque?

The maximum shear stress is 31.8 MPa, assuming pure torsion.

Using the solid circular shaft formula, τmax = 16T / (πd³), with torque in N·mm and diameter in mm.

PracticeMounting plate · CSWA style
My mass is far higher than the expected answer. What did I miss?

Your material is the issue: Part1 is assigned Plain Carbon Steel instead of the specified 6061 Alloy. Steel is much denser, and your model’s volume matches the practice part, so the geometry isn’t what’s driving the high mass.

Design intentBracket.SLDPRT
Will this part still rebuild if the main dimensions change?

Not reliably: Mounting Hole fails when the 40 mm Base Profile dimension is reduced by 20%. The other five driving dimensions rebuilt at both 20% larger and smaller, and all values are restored.

I couldn’t inspect the hole’s references, but I’d first check whether its position leaves enough material when the plate narrows.

Design intentBracket.SLDPRT
The bore must stay centered on the plate when the length changes. How should it be constrained?

Constrain the bore center to the midpoint of a construction diagonal between opposite plate corners. That keeps it centered as the plate’s length or width changes.

Design intent

Models that survive the next change.

A part that rebuilds today can still fall apart when someone changes a size. Caddie explains what drives what in your model and helps you build the intent in, so changes go where you expect.

See what drives the size

Caddie lists the dimensions that control the part by name and value, and tells you which ones only position features.

Link sizes with equations

Say the rule in plain words. Caddie confirms which dimensions you mean before it links them with an equation.

Make the wall thickness one tenth of the height.

Keep features where they belong

A bore that must stay centered should be tied to the middle, not to one edge. Caddie checks how it's located and fixes the relation.

The bore must stay centered on the plate when the length changes.
Stress test

A design intent stress test. Ask "Will this part still rebuild if the main dimensions change?" Caddie makes each driving dimension 20% bigger, then 20% smaller, rebuilds, tells you exactly what breaks, and puts every value back. On a test part it checked 17 dimensions in under 7 seconds.

Privacy

What leaves your PC, and what doesn't.

Caddie is built for engineers who work on confidential designs, so here is exactly where your data goes.

Stays on your PC

Conversation history, file notes and settings. Your SolidWorks files are never uploaded. History can be turned off or deleted in Settings.

Sent to answer you

Your question, the model's details, any image you attach and a screenshot of SolidWorks go to OpenAI through Caddie's relay. Screenshots can be turned off.

Not used for training

OpenAI doesn't train on API data, and Caddie asks it not to store conversations. OpenAI may keep requests for up to 30 days to check for abuse.

No telemetry

No account, usage tracking or analytics. Answers you rate stay on your PC unless you choose to send a problem report.

FAQ

Questions engineers ask.

Is it really free?

Yes, during the alpha. There's no account, no trial and no card. You don't need your own OpenAI account or key either.

Which SolidWorks versions does it work with?

SolidWorks 2022 and newer on 64-bit Windows 10 or 11. Caddie is tested most on SolidWorks 2026. If something doesn't work on your version, click Copy a problem report in Settings and email it to us.

Does it upload my SolidWorks files?

No. To answer, Caddie sends your question, details of the open model (names, dimensions, materials, errors and so on) and, unless you turn it off, a screenshot of the SolidWorks window. The files themselves never leave your PC.

Can it change my model without asking?

No. Every change is shown first, and nothing happens until you click Do it. You can tick "Don't ask again this session" for routine changes, but deleting features and sketch relations always asks.

Will it help me prepare for SolidWorks certification?

Yes, for the CSWA (Associate), the CSWP (Professional) and the advanced Sheet Metal exam: practice parts with checked answers, hints and quick quizzes, including quizzes on the other advanced topics. It won't help during a real exam, and you shouldn't use it there. The certification has to be your own work.

Where do the practice parts come from?

They're original parts written for Caddie, in the style of the exams, not copies of exam questions. Each answer key is computed exactly from the part's geometry, and every part was built in SolidWorks, twice at different sizes, to check that the keys match.

I'm still learning SolidWorks. Is it for me?

Yes. Tell Caddie you're learning and it explains why as well as what, says what terms like over-defined mean, and walks you through the clicks so you learn them. If you use SolidWorks every day, choose that instead: answers get shorter, and fixes are offered for you to approve. You can switch any time in Settings.

What AI does it use?

OpenAI's models, through a small relay run by Caddie that adds the API key. Caddie picks the model; you don't need to configure anything.

Can it do engineering calculations?

Yes. For stresses, deflections, bolt preloads and the like, Caddie works the numbers out with an exact calculator instead of estimating them, and says what it assumed. Treat the result as a check, not a substitute for proper analysis or sign-off.

Does it work offline?

No. Caddie needs an internet connection to answer. Your history and settings are still on your PC when you're offline.

How do updates work?

Caddie tells you inside SolidWorks when a new version is out. Download it, close SolidWorks and run the installer. Your settings and history carry over.

What about Fusion, Onshape or other CAD?

Not today. Caddie is built for SolidWorks.
