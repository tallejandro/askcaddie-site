How it works

It reads the model, so you don't have to explain it.

A general chatbot only knows what you type or paste into it. Caddie reads SolidWorks directly every time you ask, so "help" is enough of a question.

1

It reads the open document

In a fraction of a second, before it answers:

  • The feature tree, with rebuild errors and warnings
  • Sketch status, relations and what's over-defining a sketch
  • Dimensions, equations and configurations
  • Material, mass and centre of mass
  • Sheet metal settings, weldment cut lists and Hole Wizard holes
  • Assembly components and mates, drawing sheets and views
  • A screenshot of the window, unless you turn screenshots off
2

You ask in your own words

Typos and vague questions are fine. "sketch 2 red why" and "why is there a yellow triangle" both get a specific answer, because Caddie checks the model before it replies instead of asking you to describe it.

3

It explains, shows you, or does it

Answers are short and name the feature and the value. When there's work to do, Caddie starts a guide that highlights each click, or proposes the exact change for you to approve.

What it does

Help that ends with the model fixed, not just explained.

Show me

It points at the exact button, menu or field.

Ask how to do something and Caddie starts a short guide. Each step highlights where to click in SolidWorks, and ticks itself off when SolidWorks shows it's done, so you're never hunting through menus.

  • Highlights ribbon buttons, menus, PropertyManager fields and FeatureManager items
  • Moves to the next step on its own as you work
  • Show me, Skip, or stop the guide at any time

Do it

It can make the fix, after you say yes.

When the fix is clear, Caddie shows exactly what it will change. Nothing happens until you click Do it, and most changes come with an Undo button.

  • Sketches, extrudes and cuts, fillets, chamfers, shells, mirrors and patterns
  • Dimensions, materials, names, suppression and custom properties
  • If any step fails, everything is put back

Asked "sketch 2 red why", Caddie found the Vertical relation fighting the others, asked to delete it, and then explained what had been wrong.

Attach an image

Show it a drawing, sketch or photo.

Paste, drop or attach an image of what you want to model. Caddie works out the shape and the dimensions, says what it assumed, and either builds it in steps you approve or guides you through building it yourself.

  • Drawings, hand sketches, screenshots and photos
  • Says what it assumed, so you can correct it
  • You approve each step before it's built

From this drawing, Caddie built the profile, the extrude and the three holes, and finished with: "I assumed the two base holes are symmetric and the upright hole is centred across the 80 mm width, as shown."

Real answers

Specific answers, word for word.

Unedited answers from Caddie's test runs on sample models. Each one names the feature and the number, and points out what's worth checking.

Sheet metalCover.SLDPRT
how deep are the threads on these tapped holes and what size

The four holes in M6 Tapped Hole1 are M6 × 1.0, with a specified thread depth of 10 mm. The Ø5 mm tap-drill holes are blind, 12 mm deep.

That depth is worth checking: the cover is only 1.5 mm thick, so you won’t get 10 mm of thread unless there’s a thicker boss or insert at those locations.

WeldmentsFrame.SLDPRT
how long are the square tubes and how many of each do i need to cut

You need 8 pieces of 30 × 30 × 2.60 mm square tube:

  • 4 at 630 mm, with 45° mitres at both ends.
  • 4 at 970 mm, with square cuts at both ends.

Those are finished cut lengths; allow extra stock for saw kerf.

Sheet metalCover.SLDPRT
what size blank do i need to cut for this

You need a 182.6 × 95 mm blank in 1.5 mm AISI 304 stainless steel. That’s the flat-pattern bounding size with both bends accounted for using your current K-factor of 0.44.

Use the flat-pattern DXF for the actual cut outline and holes, and confirm the bend allowance with your fabricator before cutting.

PracticeMounting plate · CSWA
why is mine wrong

Your part is about 0.9% too heavy, which points to one missing Ø10 corner hole or one that doesn’t cut all the way through. The extra material matches one full corner hole, and your centre of gravity is slightly off-centre too. Check Sketch2 and Cut-Extrude1 for all four corner holes cutting through the plate.

Design intentBracket.SLDPRT
what controls the overall size of this part

Base Profile controls the part’s footprint: D1@Base Profile is 60 mm and D2@Base Profile is 40 mm. Boss-Extrude1 controls its thickness through D1@Boss-Extrude1, currently 25 mm.

Those are the three main overall-size controls; Mounting Hole controls the cut, not the outside size.

Design intent

Models that survive the next change.

A part that rebuilds today can still fall apart when someone changes a size. Caddie explains what drives what in your model and helps you build the intent in, so changes go where you expect.

See what drives the size

Caddie lists the dimensions that control the part by name and value, and tells you which ones only position features.

Link sizes with equations

Say the rule in plain words. Caddie confirms which dimensions you mean before it links them with an equation.

Keep features where they belong

A bore that must stay centred should be tied to the middle, not to one edge. Caddie checks how it's located and fixes the relation.

Privacy

What leaves your PC, and what doesn't.

Caddie is built for engineers who work on confidential designs, so here is exactly where your data goes.

Stays on your PC

Conversation history, file notes and settings. Your SolidWorks files are never uploaded. History can be turned off or deleted in Settings.

Sent to answer you

Your question, the model's details, any image you attach and a screenshot of SolidWorks go to OpenAI through Caddie's relay. Screenshots can be turned off.

Not used for training

OpenAI doesn't train on API data, and Caddie asks it not to store conversations. OpenAI may keep requests for up to 30 days to check for abuse.

No telemetry

No account, usage tracking or analytics. Answers you rate stay on your PC unless you choose to send a problem report.

FAQ

Questions engineers ask.

Is it really free?

Yes, during the alpha. There's no account, no trial and no card. You don't need your own OpenAI account or key either.

Which SolidWorks versions does it work with?

SolidWorks 2022 and newer on 64-bit Windows 10 or 11. Caddie is tested most on SolidWorks 2026. If something doesn't work on your version, click Copy a problem report in Settings and email it to us.

Does it upload my SolidWorks files?

No. To answer, Caddie sends your question, details of the open model (names, dimensions, materials, errors and so on) and, unless you turn it off, a screenshot of the SolidWorks window. The files themselves never leave your PC.

Can it change my model without asking?

No. Every change is shown first, and nothing happens until you click Do it. You can tick "Don't ask again this session" for routine changes, but deleting features and sketch relations always asks.

Will it help me pass the CSWA or CSWP?

It's a good way to prepare: practice parts with checked answers, hints and timed drills. It won't help during a real exam, and you shouldn't use it there. The certification has to be your own work.

What AI does it use?

OpenAI's models, through a small relay run by Caddie that adds the API key. Caddie picks the model; you don't need to configure anything.

Does it work offline?

No. Caddie needs an internet connection to answer. Your history and settings are still on your PC when you're offline.

How do updates work?

Caddie tells you inside SolidWorks when a new version is out. Download it, close SolidWorks and run the installer. Your settings and history carry over.

What about Fusion, Onshape or other CAD?

Not today. Caddie is built for SolidWorks.
